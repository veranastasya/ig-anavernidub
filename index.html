What the numbers say

Reach has fully reverted, and the account underneath it is in better shape than it was. 9,111 people, roughly the late-August baseline and a tenth of the viral week. But views fell 45% while interactions fell only 10%, and 6.6% of everyone reached engaged — the highest rate this dashboard has recorded. Followers held flat at 2,153. What is left after three reach events is a smaller, more interested audience.

The best thing this week is the apps Reel, and almost nobody saw it. Three named apps, 640 people, 7 saves — 1.1% of everyone reached kept it, the best save rate on this page. That is the fourth time the pattern has appeared: name specific things a person can go and find, and your audience saves it. The DC day guide, the travel kit, the Paris swaps, now this. Every one of them reached under 700 people.

So the real problem is now precise. The posts that distribute are jokes, and they convert at somewhere between one follow per 575 and one per 2,157 with nothing in the data predicting which. The posts that people actually keep are specific lists, and Instagram shows them to 600 people. You are not short of either ingredient; they have never once been in the same post.

The Wellio carousel is the first real test of the product story. Two previous posts touching the tracking idea reached 2,571 and 667 and converted nothing. This one explains what Wellio is, and at two hours it has 59 From Other views, which is a better start than any carousel since early October. It needs until Thursday before it means anything.

What that means for the next post. Try putting the list inside the joke. The apps Reel earned saves and no reach; the massage-therapist Reel earned reach and three saves. One post that opens with the joke and pays off with three named things is the obvious experiment, and it has not been run yet.
