What the numbers say

Two different machines, and you're running both. Reels reached 78–86% non-followers; carousels reached 4–37%. Reels are how strangers find you. Carousels are how the people who already follow you stay warm. Neither is better — but judging a carousel by views, or a Reel by engagement rate, will make you optimize the wrong thing. The Carrie Bradshaw post has the best engagement rate of the week (21.6%) and produced zero new followers.

One post did almost all the acquisition work. The DMV gym roundup drove 5 of the week's 8 follows and 31 of the profile visits, off the smallest reach of any Reel. It's the only creative that gave someone something to take away. Every other post asked for a reaction; that one answered a question.

Saves are near zero across the board. Four saves on the gym roundup, three each on two Reels, zero on everything else. Saves are the strongest signal that content is worth returning to, and nothing you published this week qualified — including an NYC itinerary, which is the exact format people normally save. The slides are photos rather than a referenceable list.

Stories are quietly your largest channel. 53% of views and 46% of interactions came from Stories, not from anything in the grid. That's the half of your output this dashboard measures least well, and it's the half your existing audience actually sees.

Nothing this week pointed at Wellio. All six creatives are lifestyle and travel. The week was a genuine reach win — views nearly doubled, interactions more than doubled — but it built an audience for New York content, not for a client-management platform for coaches. Worth deciding deliberately whether that's the trade you want, rather than letting the algorithm pick for you: the format that converts (a useful, specific roundup) is the same format that would work for coaching content.
