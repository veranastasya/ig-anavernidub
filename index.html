What the numbers say

The carousel converted again, and this time the comparison is clean. The DC itinerary reached 391 people — the smallest of anything this week — and produced 2 follows and 20 profile visits. Your Aug 17 NYC itinerary was the same idea, the same format, roughly the same reach, and produced nothing. The difference: this one tagged three venues and ended on a question. That is the third carousel in a row to convert, and the first time the "tag someone, ask something" pattern has been tested against a matched control.

Meanwhile the big Reel is still at zero, four days in. 3,956 accounts, 31 shares, no follows, not one profile visit. Reach and outcomes are now clearly independent on this account: feed posts are 0.2% of your views this week and produced half your follows.

The week’s totals look worse than the week actually was. Views fell 25% and profile visits halved, but both are mechanical — the boosted carousel finished aging out of the window, taking its 41 paid profile visits with it. Interactions barely moved (537 vs 572) on a quarter fewer views, which is a real improvement in quality even if the headline looks bad.

One caveat on the DC post, and it matters for where this is going. It converted, but it is a travel guide. The two people it recruited are interested in Washington, not in coaching. The same is true of the DMV gym roundup’s five follows — those at least were fitness. If the aim is an audience that contains coaches, the format is now proven and the subject is the remaining variable: run the identical structure (a specific roundup, venues or people tagged, a question at the end) on a training or coaching topic and see whether it converts as well.

Cadence held this week and it shows. Four posts in the window instead of one. Followers stopped falling and ticked up to 2,109. Small, but it is the first upward move in a week.
