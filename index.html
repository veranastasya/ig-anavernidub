What the numbers say

Three days on, the breakout Reel has still produced nothing. 4,843 views, 3,942 accounts reached, 31 shares — and zero follows, zero profile visits. Instagram is pushing a "this post is on fire, make it an ad" prompt at you for it. That prompt is worth ignoring: paying to show more people a post that converts nobody buys more of the same nothing.

The only converter left the window today, and you can see the hole it leaves. The boosted carousel rolled out after seven days, and profile visits fell 34% to 180. It was responsible for most of them. Followers have been flat at 2,108 for three days. The account currently has no post in its active window that produces a follower except the Pilates Reel, which leaves tomorrow.

Six weeks of evidence now says one thing. Entertainment travels and converts nobody; the industry-commentary Reel converted twice off a third of the reach; the carousel converted eight times off a tenth of it. The ranking has never changed, only the sample size. What has changed is that the reach problem is definitively solved — 3,942 strangers in three days, without spending anything.

So the work is entirely downstream of reach now. Two things, in order. First the profile itself: someone arriving from a night-out Reel currently finds no statement of who you are or what you are building, and that is the page every one of those 3,942 people would have landed on. Second, give the entertaining Reels one line — on screen or spoken — that makes a stranger curious about the account rather than just the clip. Neither of those is a content-calendar problem; both are a positioning problem, and they are cheap to fix.

Today’s post is the one thing to check tomorrow, once its full metrics are readable — 49 interactions on 863 views is the best early ratio since the Pilates Reel.
