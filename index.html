What the numbers say

The boost stopped after one day. This morning it read "4 days remaining" on a $50 budget; tonight it reads Completed, Duration 1 day, $11.49 spent. If that was not deliberate, it is the first thing to check in Ad tools. What it bought: 41 profile visits and 6 follows at $0.28 each, delivered 67.6% into England.

The Pilates post is the most interesting thing to happen on this account in six weeks. Not because of its size — 1,476 views is the smallest of the five — but because of who is engaging. 48.9% of its interactions came from non-followers, against 28.1% for the Netflix Reel and 38.4% for "NY, but". It picked up 2 saves and 2 shares in fourteen hours; the Netflix Reel has 1 save after three days. Strangers are not just watching it, they are keeping it.

The two comedy Reels are finished, and the ledger is brutal. Between them they reached 5,900 accounts and produced two followers. The Netflix Reel set an account record for reach (3,135) and has the worst engagement rate on the page (2.8%). Reach at that quality is not an asset; it is a number.

Carousels convert, Reels do not, and the gap is no longer arguable. Eight organic follows off ~324 people reached for the boosted carousel. Three follows off ~9,700 reached for the four Reels combined. Six weeks of this dashboard have said the same thing in smaller print.

What to do with that. The two signals point at one experiment: the Pilates post proved industry commentary engages non-followers, and the carousel format is the only thing that converts them. Neither has been tried with the other. A carousel that takes a real position on the coaching business — the kind of thing you would say in a discovery interview — with a question in the caption, is the single test that would tell you whether this account can build a coach audience or only a lifestyle one. Everything else here is already answered.
