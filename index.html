What the numbers say

The pivot to fitness content has happened, and the fitness content is not being distributed. Four of this week’s posts are structured advice: shorter workouts, tracking your lifts, three ways to stay consistent, a travel wellness kit. The three that have had time to run reached 430, 389 and 428 people. The two jokes in the same week reached 6,797 and 2,448. That gap is not about quality — it is the same signature every explainer on this account has produced since August, and it is now eleven posts deep.

A correction on last week’s call. This page flagged the Oct 6 Reel at nine comments in 90 minutes and said comments were the signal worth watching. It finished with ten comments and 124 shares. The conversation did not continue; the post travelled on shares like every other large Reel here. The early-comment reading was wrong, and share rate remains the only early number that has ever predicted reach on this account.

The travel wellness carousel is the quiet success. 428 people, three saves, and twelve profile visits — more than any carousel this dashboard has recorded. Two weeks ago the carousel built on your own trip numbers got zero comments, zero saves and five profile visits off a near-identical audience. The difference is that this one lists specific, named things a person could go and buy. Specificity, not personal data, is what makes your audience move.

Engagement held while reach fell. Views are down 28% but interactions only 4%, profile visits are up 39%, and 6.4% of everyone reached engaged — the highest rate on this page. The audience that remains after the travel bump is more interested than the one the trip brought in. That is a better base to build on than the raw numbers suggest.

What that means for the next post. The advice content is right for where the account is going and wrong in its current construction — a tip list delivered straight reaches 400 people every time. The two things that do travel are jokes and specific named lists. The next fitness post should be one of those two shapes: a joke whose punchline is the advice, or a list of named, findable things. Writing a better tip will not fix a distribution problem.
